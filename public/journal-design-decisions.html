Web design for small business: start from the decision path, not the template

A design process that maps how buyers decide before it decides colours.

Oct 14, 20266 min readBy Antony S.

Design that starts from a mood board often looks polished and performs badly. The stronger starting point is a mapped conversion path: what a visitor sells, who it is for, what it costs, and what happens next.

What a visitor actually needs to decide

Every credible quote covers the same four kinds of work, even if the invoice does not say so:

  • Thinking — audience, offer, structure, and the pages the site actually needs.
  • Design — layout, type, colour and reusable components, not a purchased theme.
  • Build — responsive markup, performance, accessibility, forms and structured data.
  • Handover — content migration, redirects, analytics, training and ownership of the code.

When a quote looks unusually cheap, one of those four is usually missing — most often handover, which is why so many businesses end up locked into a maintenance contract just to change a phone number.

A website is not an expense with a shelf life. It is an asset that should be faster, cheaper and easier to run every year you own it.

The design decision flow

Start with task mapping

Five pages, one clear conversion path, mobile-first design and foundational technical SEO. This is the right bracket for a new business or a café, salon or tradesperson who needs to look credible and be found on a phone.

Move to component library

Up to ten pages, a journal or project system, local SEO, AI-search readiness and a lead capture setup. This is where most ambitious small businesses land: they publish regularly and want the site to earn its keep.

Approve responsive states

Custom web applications, e-commerce, booking flows and AI automation. The budget goes into logic and integrations rather than page count — and the scope should be written down before anyone starts.

Accessibility and performance are not afterthoughts

Domain registration and hosting are usually excluded from project prices, and that is normal. The domain should be registered in your name, and hosting should be billed separately so you can move if you ever want to. Ask who owns the account before signing anything.

Handover is part of design

Compare the scope, not the headline number. Ask for the page list, what happens after launch, who owns the code and what the monthly costs are. A €790 site with no handover is more expensive than a €1,490 site you can edit yourself.
